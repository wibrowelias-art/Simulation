# Research notes: how real online communities talk

These notes drive the generators in `src/lib/sim/`. Every character, server, outlet and post in GOD is fictional. The research is only used for *statistical shape* (length, rhythm, style). No real person's content is copied.

## 1. Message length
- Discord chat messages are short. A community-analytics write-up puts the **average at about 7 words per message**, and new or low-engagement users average about **4** ([CommunityOne / ModX](https://luyilousia.medium.com/modx-bot-part-1-4-charts-to-track-your-discord-operations-369de940b921)).
- One user's 7-year Discord export: 720,259 words over 102,556 messages, again **≈7 words/message**. About 9% of messages had attachments ([bwhitta.com](https://bwhitta.com/discord-data-analysis)).
- A large anonymised dataset that merges self-replies shows a mean of 19 words per *exchange*, with a long right tail (skew ≈ 6) ([Discord-Dialogues, Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mookiezi/Discord-Dialogues)).
- **Generator rule:** sample a target length from a right-skewed (log-normal-ish) distribution with a median of about 6 words. Many messages are 1–3 words ("real", "LMAO", "wait what"). Occasionally someone writes a paragraph-long rant (analysts, haters, conspiracists).

## 2. Style: casing, punctuation, slang, emoji
- Gen Z texting style is mostly **no capitals and no full stops**, with heavy irony. Common shorthand: "fr / fr fr", "ong", "ngl", "bet", "mid", "no cap", "lowkey", "istg", "iykyk", "icl" (UK). **💀 has largely replaced 😂** for laughing, and 😭 can mean laughing *or* overwhelmed. 😂 and 👍 read as older or passive-aggressive ([Kaiwa guide](https://trykaiwa.com/blog/english-texting-shortcuts-guide-2026), [FunkyEnglish](https://funkyenglish.com/text-slang-meanings/), [OneAppleFall decoder](https://oneapplefall.com/ai-tools/text-slang-decoder/)).
- Reaction-emoji vocabulary: 💀 "I'm dead", 😭, 🔥, 🐐 (GOAT), 👀 (gossip), 🧢 (lie), 🤡 (fool), 💯 ([Internet Matters dictionary](https://www.internetmatters.org/resources/text-dictionary/), [FunkyEnglish](https://funkyenglish.com/text-slang-meanings/)).
- ALL CAPS means shouting or hype.
- **Generator rule:** each character gets a fixed *voice* (lowercase-zoomer, caps-hype, proper, millennial with 😂/lol, terse, boomer with "..." and full sentences, formal-analyst, emoji-heavy). Each also gets a personal slang set, 1–3 signature emojis, a typo rate and a catchphrase, so the same person sounds the same across days. Older characters (35+) skew to proper/boomer/millennial voices, younger ones to zoomer/lowercase.

## 3. Who talks: lurkers vs. power users
- Participation inequality: the **90-9-1 rule** says 90% lurk, 9% contribute sometimes and 1% create most content ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/participation-inequality/)). It is a rule of thumb ([ManagingCommunities](https://www.managingcommunities.com/2012/07/19/90-9-1-rule-and-participation-inequality/)). On Twitter, about **3 in 4** users were lurkers ([ACM WWW'19 companion](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3308560.3316705)). Post counts fit a **Zipf / power curve** (98.6% variance explained) ([Internet Interventions 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.invent.2014.09.003)).
- **Generator rule:** characters get an activity tier. About 70% are lurkers (weight 0.15), 22% regulars (weight 1) and 8% power users (weight 6–12), plus a Zipf-like jitter. Server member counts shown in the UI are much larger than the speaking cast, as in a real server.

## 4. When they talk: daily rhythm and timezones
- Twitter activity is lowest at night (**trough ≈ 04:30 local**), rises to a lunchtime peak, dips in the late afternoon and peaks again in the evening ([PMC 6590897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590897/)). Busiest hours have about **3× the volume** of the quietest ([Pingdom](https://www.pingdom.com/blog/in-depth-study-of-twitter-how-much-we-tweet-and-when/)).
- Users split into **morning types (peak ≈ 09:30) and evening types (peak ≈ 23:15)**, plus intermediate types ([Scientific Reports 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-69447-8)).
- During the day, total volume changes mostly because each account posts more, and @mentions/replies rise in the evening ([Bruns et al., "One Day in the Life of a National Twittersphere"](https://snurb.info/files/2019/One%20Day%20in%20the%20Life%20of%20a%20National%20Twittersphere.pdf)).
- **Generator rule:** every character has a home timezone from their location and a chronotype (morning, evening or night-owl). Their chance of being online follows a bimodal daily curve in *their* local time. Global volume therefore follows who is awake: European evenings and US evenings are busy, and the hours around 09:00–11:00 UTC are quieter.

## 5. How they talk: bursts and threads, not independent lines
- Human communication is **bursty**: quick exchanges separated by long silences, with heavy-tailed gaps between messages ([Barabási, Nature 2005](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature03459)). Starting a conversation looks roughly random (Poisson), but the replies *inside* a conversation come in fast bursts ([Wu et al., PNAS 2010](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1013140107)).
- Discord logs are full of reply chains and self-follow-ups, where one author fires off several short messages in a row. Datasets often merge these "self-replies" ([Discord-Dialogues](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mookiezi/Discord-Dialogues), [discord-message-extractor](https://github.com/rickeev/discord-message-extractor)).
- Channels are unequal: #general carries most of the traffic, and niche channels are quiet ([CommunityOne](https://luyilousia.medium.com/modx-bot-part-1-4-charts-to-track-your-discord-operations-369de940b921)).
- **Generator rule:** conversations start at a Poisson rate, scaled by the daily curve, topic heat and channel weight (general ≫ niche). Each one becomes an *episode*: an opener, then replies that quote or ping earlier speakers, with log-normal gaps of 2–90 s. Episodes include self-follow-up bursts ("wait", "nvm", "*typo fix"), pile-ons when the opener is spicy, friends backing each other, rivals arguing, occasional topic drift to world news or someone's personal life, and a moderator stepping in when the thread gets heated. Bot messages (level-ups, starboard) appear now and then.

## 6. Usernames and display names
- Discord's current system: a **unique, forced-lowercase @username**, 2–32 characters, limited to a–z, 0–9, `_` and `.` (no consecutive periods), plus a free-form **display name** with capitals, spaces, emoji and non-Latin characters that can change any time. Servers can also set per-server nicknames ([Discord Support](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/12620128861463-New-Usernames-Display-Names), [Discord blog](https://discord.com/blog/usernames/), [GamerBolt rules summary](https://www.gamerbolt.com/discord-username-rules/)).
- **Generator rule:** handles mix the common patterns: `word_word`, `wordword`, `word.word`, `name` plus a birth year or short number, letter-swaps (`k1ra`), aesthetic soft words (`lunarsoftie`), `not`/`its`/`the`/`real` prefixes, legacy `xX_name_Xx` styles (only as display names now, since handles are lowercase), and doubled letters (`vvoid`). Display names differ from handles: capitalised real-ish first names, an emoji suffix, all-lowercase, or "name | tag".

## 7. Profile pictures
- Anime-style avatars are a dominant style. Pets, aesthetic/soft edits, Y2K icons, minimal icons, funny reaction images and matching avatars are also common. Small circular crops favour simple, high-contrast subjects ([Kuromi PFP guide](https://kuromipfp.com/cute-pfp-for-discord/)). Many accounts keep the **default avatar**, which moderators also associate with throwaway or bot accounts ([r/discordapp thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/discordapp/comments/v8fdrd/the_new_discord_default_profile_pictures/)).
- **Generator rule:** weighted styles: original anime-style illustrated faces 24%, default blob avatar 14%, pets (cat/dog/frog) 14%, aesthetic scenery 12%, pixel-art characters 9%, reaction/meme faces 8%, game-style helmet/robot 8%, monogram/initial 6%, abstract 5%. All are **procedurally drawn SVGs**: no photos of real people and no copyrighted characters or logos. Haters skew to default or meme pfps, crypto people to pixel/NFT-style, and influencers to aesthetic avatars.

## 8. Moderators and bots
- Moderators speak in a short, calm, procedural tone ("keep it civil", "moving this to #memes", "last warning"). Bots post formulaic messages (level-ups, polls, starboard).
- **Generator rule:** moderators come from the server's regulars, and they step in during heated episodes and on spam. Fictional bots (`Tally`, `Starboard+`) post templated system lines that are visually marked as bots.
